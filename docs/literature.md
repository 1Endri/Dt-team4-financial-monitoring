### #5 Görög (2009)
- What: Model-development study using selected earned value indicators to build a contractor cash-flow planning and control model across activities, time periods, and cumulative performance, illustrated with an example relevant to lump-sum contracts (reviewed via publisher preview).
- Found: Traditional earned value analysis alone does not fully address contractor cash-flow control; pricing and margin forecasts should account for project costs, cash deficits, and associated financing costs.
- For FAUnance: Key prior work for indicator design, motivating us to display cost variance, project cash shortages (receipts, payments, and projected negative balances), and forecast margin separately rather than merging them into one metric.

### #6 Willems & Vanhoucke (2015)
- What: Classification review of project control and EVM articles across selected journals, categorizing studies along six dimensions (research problem, contribution, methodology, analysis, validation, and application) to map trends and research gaps (reviewed via publisher preview).
- Found: Highlights four future directions: accounting for project uncertainty, stronger validation via historical data or simulation, integrating time and cost with factors such as quality and sustainability, and developing and validating corrective-action procedures.
- For FAUnance: Frames our literature positioning (comparing what existing tools monitor, forecast, validate, and act on) and motivates structured evaluation of our demo—testing calculation agreement, exception handling, and user tasks against current spreadsheets.

### #7 Hazır (2015)
- What: Review of analytical models, methods, and decision support tools for project monitoring and control, covering earned value analysis, optimization models, decision support systems (DSS), and integration with project management software.
- Found: Concludes that project DSS should be model-driven, act as early-warning systems, integrate with commonly used PM software, offer understandable interfaces, enable what-if analysis, and support the timing and choice of corrective actions.
- For FAUnance: Provides design rationale for deviation detection, early-warning risk flags, reviewable exception and approval workflows, integration with existing project data, and future what-if scenario comparisons (though review recommendations alone do not validate our system).

### #8 Flyvbjerg, Holm & Buhl (2002)
- What: Historical empirical study of 258 transport infrastructure projects in 20 countries, comparing estimated construction costs at the decision-to-build stage with actual completion costs across project types, geographies, and time.
- Found: Actual costs exceeded estimates in 86% of the sample (27.6% average overrun) with no significant decline over roughly 70 years; the authors favor strategic misrepresentation as an explanation while acknowledging intentional deception is not conclusively proven.
- For FAUnance: Establishes the problem background motivating immutable original budgets and traceable deviation tracking against labelled baseline versions, though infrastructure overruns alone do not prove that a monitoring tool reduces overruns or delivers a given ROI.

## B — EBITDA critique

### #9 Bouwens, de Kok & Verriest (2019)
- What: Evaluates EBITDA as a performance measure and studies its use in 15,895 annual reports and 51,758 earnings releases of S&P 1500 firms (2005–2016).
- Found: EBITDA is not defined in GAAP/IFRS, ignores working capital and is usually the highest number (paints a "rosy picture"); firms that emphasize it tend to be smaller, more leveraged, more capital-intensive and less profitable.
- For FAUnance: We use EBITDA only as an internal project-control metric with one fixed definition (no adjustments), shown next to cash flow and working capital, never as the only number.
